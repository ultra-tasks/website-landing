Privacy policy,
plainly said.

What Ultra Tasks collects, what it does not, and what we cannot see. One policy covers this website, the apps you download, and the optional UltraSync sync service.

By Adrian Chase · Updated 17 November 2026

In short

  • The apps have no analytics, no ads and no tracking. Your tasks stay on your device, and the apps need no account.
  • This website counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics. That is the only analytics we run anywhere.
  • If you choose to create an UltraSync account, we store your email address, encrypted account data and the account and security details listed below. Optional public calendar feeds are a separate, readable export.
  • We cannot read your encrypted task sync data. It is encrypted on your device before it leaves, and we never hold the keys. That also means we cannot recover your login credentials, so keep your recovery key safe.
  • If you publish a calendar feed, its event data is readable by the server and anyone with its secret link. This is optional and requires your consent; see Public calendar feeds.
  • App stores, GitHub and the services you connect yourself have their own policies. We say where they come into play.

Who this covers

This policy applies to:

  • This website, ultratasks.app, including the download page and the guides.
  • The Ultra Tasks apps for Windows, macOS, Linux and Android, the web app at app.ultratasks.app, and the iPhone and iPad apps once they are released.
  • UltraSync, the optional sync service we run at app.ultratasks.app, including its account dashboard.

It does not cover servers run by other people (you can host UltraSync yourself, and whoever runs that server sets its policy), the stores you download from, GitHub, or services you connect to the app yourself. Ultra Tasks is independently maintained by Adrian Chase, who is responsible for the personal data described here.

This website

  • Visit counting. We use Cloudflare Web Analytics to count visits. Your browser loads a small script from Cloudflare and reports each page view. Cloudflare says it uses no cookies or localStorage and does not fingerprint visitors for analytics. This is the only analytics we use.
  • Hosting. Cloudflare serves the site, so it handles each request for a page, including your IP address, as any web host does.
  • Downloads. A Download button opens an address on ultratasks.app that sends your browser on to GitHub. GitHub then serves the file, and its own privacy statement applies to that request.
  • Nothing else. The pages set no cookies, show no ads, have no sign-up forms, and load no outside fonts or scripts apart from the visit counter above. The Sign in link opens the UltraSync dashboard, which is covered below.

The apps

The apps have no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking. You do not need an account to use them. Your tasks, projects, notes and settings are stored on your own device, and we have no access to them.

What the apps may contact

  • An update check, on desktop only. The Windows, macOS and Linux apps ask GitHub for the number of the latest release shortly after they start and then once a day. The request carries no account, identifier or task data, but GitHub sees your IP address as it does for any web request. Turn it off in Settings (look for “Automatically check for new versions”). Builds from the Microsoft Store, the Mac App Store and the Snap Store skip the check because the store updates them. The Android, iPhone, iPad and web versions never make it.
  • The web app. The web app at app.ultratasks.app needs no account and keeps your tasks, projects, notes and settings in your browser, on your device. It does not send them to us. Clearing the site’s data in your browser erases them, so export a backup from Settings now and then. The server that delivers the app’s files sees your IP address and the time of each request, as any web server does; we add no analytics to it.
  • Sync services you turn on. If you connect UltraSync, WebDAV or Nextcloud, Dropbox or OneDrive, your device talks to that service directly. For services other than ours, their privacy policies apply and we never receive the data.
  • Integrations and plugins you set up. They contact the service you point them at, such as an issue tracker or a calendar, using the details you enter. We are not a party to those connections. Google Calendar has its own section, below.
  • App stores. If you install from a store, the store collects its own information about installs, updates and, depending on your settings, crashes, under its own privacy policy, and may share aggregate figures with us. We add no analytics of our own to the apps, and a store does not give us your tasks.
  • Android. The Standard Android build includes Google’s Play services libraries, and anything Google collects through them falls under Google’s privacy policy. The F-Droid build does not include them.

Google Calendar and Google user data (optional)

You can connect your Google account so Ultra Tasks can show your Google Calendar events next to your plan. This is optional, and the apps work fully without it. You sign in on Google’s own page, and Google asks you to approve each permission.

What the app asks Google for

  • Calendar events (calendar.events): to read events from the calendars you choose and show them, and, only if you turn on a feature that writes to a calendar, such as time blocks, to create or update events on the calendar you pick.
  • Your list of calendars (calendar.calendarlist.readonly): so you can choose which calendars to show.

Where Google data goes

  • Your device talks to Google directly. Event data and the permission Google gives the app are never sent to us, and we run no server that receives them.
  • The sign-in token stays on your device. It is not part of the data you sync.
  • If you add an event as a task, it becomes an ordinary task. Like any task, it is stored on your device and goes wherever you choose to sync your tasks. With UltraSync that is encrypted on your device first, as described below.

Limited use of Google user data

Ultra Tasks’ use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. In plain terms:

  • We use Google user data only to provide the calendar features you see in the app, and we do not use it for anything else.
  • We do not sell it, and we do not use or transfer it for advertising, including personalized, retargeted or interest-based advertising.
  • We do not use it to develop, improve or train generalized artificial intelligence or machine learning models.
  • We do not transfer it to others, except as you direct by adding events to tasks you sync yourself, as needed to comply with the law, or as part of a merger or sale of the app.
  • Nobody on our side reads it. It never reaches us, so there is nothing for a person to read.

Disconnecting and deleting

In the app, open Integrations, edit your Google Calendar connection and choose Disconnect, or delete the connection. Either removes the sign-in token from your device. That does not tell Google, so to also withdraw the app’s access, remove it on your Google Account permissions page. Tasks you created from events stay until you delete them. Events the app wrote to your calendar stay in Google Calendar until you delete them there.

UltraSync accounts (optional)

You can use Ultra Tasks for as long as you like without an account. An account is only for UltraSync, our private sync service. UltraSync is in beta. Registration on the official service opened on 17 November 2026 and requires email verification. This section covers UltraSync accounts.

What we store

  • Your email address, and a display name or username if you add one. We need it to create and verify the account and to help you recover access.
  • Your encrypted account data: your encrypted key vault, and the tasks and other data you sync, in encrypted form that we cannot read.
  • Public calendar feed exports, if you enable them: readable event data and the details needed to serve and revoke each feed, as explained below.
  • Sign-in and security details: a cryptographic record that lets the server check your password without ever receiving it, the details needed to check your second factor or passkey, and one-way hashes of your recovery codes.
  • Your devices: the name of each device you sign in from and when, so you can see and revoke them in the dashboard.
  • Account dates: when you created the account and when you accepted the terms and this privacy policy.
  • Technical data: the size and timing of your encrypted records and, as with any server, your IP address and the time of each request. We use it to run the service, limit request rates and block abuse. Server logs rotate automatically.

What we cannot see

We cannot read your encrypted task sync data.

Your task sync data, including tasks, projects, notes, tags and settings, is encrypted on your device before it is sent, with keys that are created and kept on your devices. We do not have those keys, and no support tool or backup lets us decrypt that data. If you separately publish a public calendar feed, the event export is readable as described below; it does not give the server your encryption keys. Your password and your recovery key are never sent to us in readable form.

We cannot recover your login credentials. We never receive your password or your recovery key in readable form, so we cannot look them up, send them to you, or reset them in a way that brings your data back. If you forget your password, use the recovery option on the sign-in page, which needs your recovery key (and your second factor, if you use one). If you lose both your password and your recovery key, we cannot restore your data, so keep your recovery key somewhere safe.

And if an authority ever asked us for your tasks, we could not provide them. All we hold is the list above.

What encryption does not hide

  • Your email address, your device and session details, the size and timing of your encrypted records, and the IP address you connect from are visible to the server.
  • Sync encryption does not protect the copy of your tasks that each of your devices keeps for itself. Use your device’s own disk encryption for that.
  • The account dashboard is a web page our server delivers to your browser, so signing in there means trusting the page we serve.
  • UltraSync is in beta and has not yet had an independent cryptographic review.

Public calendar feeds

Publishing a calendar feed is optional and requires your consent. The app sends a readable iCalendar export to the server so calendar apps can subscribe without signing in. This export is outside the end-to-end encrypted task sync. By default, events use a generic busy title. You can choose to include task titles; task notes are not included. The 0.4.0 beta exports existing task instances, without projecting future recurring occurrences.

Anyone with the secret feed link can read the export, including a calendar provider you give it to. Treat the link as a password. Rotating it disables the previous link; revoking or deleting the feed stops future requests. Calendar apps and providers may retain copies they already downloaded, so these actions cannot erase those copies. Their own privacy policies apply.

The official service uses HTTPS, excludes feed URL paths from access and error logs, and prevents shared caching of feed responses. The server operator can read the exported events, and disaster-recovery backups can retain them until the backup rotation removes them. Publishing a feed does not make the rest of your encrypted task data readable.

How we use it

Only to run your account: to let you sign in, to verify your email address, to keep your encrypted data available on your devices, to send you verification, recovery and security messages, to protect the service from abuse, to serve calendar feeds you publish, and to tell you about important changes to it. We do not sell or rent your email address, we do not use it for advertising, and we do not analyse your task data. We cannot read your encrypted sync data; published calendar exports are readable as explained above.

Who else handles it

We run UltraSync on Oracle Cloud, which hosts the servers and database that hold the data listed above. Purelymail delivers the account emails we send you, such as verification, recovery and security messages, so it handles your email address and the content of those messages. Each handles that data only to provide its service to us. A calendar feed you publish is also available to anyone with its link. Otherwise, we share nothing else with anyone unless the law requires it.

Keeping and deleting

  • We keep your account for as long as it exists, so your devices can keep syncing.
  • You can delete your account yourself from the account dashboard. That removes your account record, device sessions, second-factor details, encrypted data and public calendar feeds from the live service right away.
  • Backups kept for disaster recovery can hold older copies until they are replaced in the normal rotation. They contain the same kinds of data as above. Encrypted task sync data stays unreadable to us; published calendar exports remain readable in backups.

If you host it yourself

You can run your own UltraSync server. Your devices then talk only to your server and we receive nothing. Whoever runs that server is responsible for its data and sets its own policy.

UltraSync connections must use HTTPS, except to a server on your own device (localhost). If you choose another sync option, such as WebDAV or Nextcloud, the app connects to the server address you enter, which can be a plain HTTP address on your own network. The protection of that connection is up to you and the server you run.

Cookies and local storage

This website sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser. The apps keep your data in their own storage on your device, which is how they work offline. The UltraSync dashboard keeps only what it needs in your browser to keep you signed in and to finish a login you started from the app. None of it is used for advertising or analytics.

Your choices and rights

Because the apps send us nothing, there is very little for us to hold about you. You can:

  • Delete your UltraSync account at any time, from the dashboard.
  • Turn off the desktop update check in Settings, or use the apps without ever connecting to anything.
  • Export your tasks from the app whenever you like. They are on your device already.
  • Ask what we hold about you, ask us to correct or delete it, or object to how we use it. Depending on where you live, laws such as the GDPR in the EU and UK give you these rights. We will respond to such requests wherever you are, once we have verified that the request really comes from you. For an account, that usually means confirming through the email address on it, and we may decline a request we cannot verify.

If you are in the EU or UK and are unhappy with how we handled a request, you can also complain to your local data protection authority.

Changes to this policy

When this policy changes we will update the date at the top. If a change affects what we collect or how we use it, we will say what changed on this page.

Contact

Questions, or a request about your data? Email hi@ultratasks.app, or open an issue on GitHub. Please do not post personal details in a public issue: say that you have a privacy request and we will arrange a private reply, or email us instead. If the request is about an account, write from the address on it. We use what you send only to answer you.
